# Interiors and finish inside

An interior starts from what happens in it, not from furniture.

## Plan before blocks

1. **Purpose.** Write one sentence per room: who is here and what they do.
2. **Route.** Draw the path from the door through the work to the exit. Keep it 2 wide, and 3 where people pass or carry on working.
3. **Service.** Everything that must be repaired or refilled needs a way to reach it.
4. **Zones.** Separate moving, working and storing. Show the change with the floor, a step, a low wall or the ceiling height.

## Vertical movement

- Stairs for routes people use daily, ladders only for rare service access.
- A mezzanine needs a guard rail on every open edge, a clear way up and headroom of at least 3 blocks above and below.
- Put a landing at the top and bottom of every flight. A door must never open onto a drop.

## Shell

- **Floor.** One main floor material, with a border or inset to mark zones. Do not scatter lights in a grid across a floor.
- **Walls.** Calmer than the exterior. Keep a base course and a line at door height, and let the structure show.
- **Ceiling.** Beams on the same module as the pilasters outside, so the inside and outside belong together.
- **Openings.** Windows seen from inside need the same care: reveal, sill and light falling onto something useful.

## Light

- Light tasks and routes first, then mood. Avoid even light everywhere.
- Hang light from beams, or hide it behind trim or in a recess. A visible source should look like a fitting.
- Keep mob-proofing in mind, but meet it with the lighting design and not with random torches.

## Scale and furniture

- Fill a big room with big things: columns, machines and platforms. Small furniture in a big hall looks lost.
- Group objects by use: a workbench with storage and light beside it. Things floating alone along the walls read as a catalogue.
- Leave empty floor. It is what makes a room feel usable.

## Industrial rooms and farms

- **Show the machine.** Frame it, and give it a viewing and service walkway with a rail. Keep clear distance from moving and blasting parts.
- **Protect what explodes or moves.** Blast-resistant lining goes where the blast goes, finished cleanly at its edges.
- **Make it readable.** Use the same material for all service platforms, and one colour or sign per collecting line.
- **Pipes and cables** run in straight groups along walls and beams, and they connect to something.
- **Keep the inside wall clean.** No stray columns or lumps where a curved wall was generated: walk the inside before calling it done.

## Review

- [ ] Can you say in one sentence what each room is for?
- [ ] Walk the route from every door. Does anything block or drop?
- [ ] Can every machine part be reached for repair?
- [ ] Do inside beams line up with outside pilasters?
- [ ] Is every light source deliberate?
